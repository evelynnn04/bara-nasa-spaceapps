How the numbers work

Hotspots, in MODIS terms

Per 0.05° cell and day, an Aqua MODIS hotspot counts once and a VIIRS hotspot counts a fraction of one:

MODIS nday + nnight

VIIRS nday / kday[r, m] + nnight / knight[r, m]

ln kd[r, m] = μd + ρd,r + σd,m

d is day or night, r the island group and m the calendar month. The record is Aqua before 1 February 2012, then one VIIRS satellite per day (S-NPP, else NOAA-20, else NOAA-21). An area's value is the sum over the cells whose centre lies inside it.

Fitting the factors

On days both Aqua (A) and VIIRS (B) observed, starting from μ = ln(ΣB / ΣA) and repeating until nothing changes by more than 10−6:

ρr = sr · ln( Br / Σm A[r, m] · eμ + σm )

σm = sm · ln( Bm / Σr A[r, m] · eμ + ρr )

s = A / (A + n0),   n0 = 2000 hotspots

Agreement between the sensors

Monthly totals on days both observed, a for Aqua and b for harmonised VIIRS:

Correlation rlog = corr( ln(1 + a), ln(1 + b) )

Typical difference median | ln(b / a) |, months with a, b ≥ 10

Bias exp( median ln(b / a) )

Converged needs rlog ≥ 0.95, a typical difference of at most 0.25 and the same peak month in at least 6 of 7 years; partial needs rlog ≥ 0.90 and at most 0.50.
