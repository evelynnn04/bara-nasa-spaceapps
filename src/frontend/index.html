A river through the rainforest

Sumatra and Kalimantan hold some of the richest rainforest on Earth. Every day and every night, two kinds of NASA instrument look down on it for fire.

Two eyes in orbit

MODIS has watched since 2000, on Terra and then Aqua. From 2012, VIIRS joined: sharper eyes on Suomi NPP, then NOAA-20 and NOAA-21.

A hotspot

To a satellite, a fire is a pixel hotter than its neighbours: about 1 km across for MODIS, 375 m for VIIRS. One fire can light one MODIS pixel and several VIIRS pixels.

4.9 times

In 2015, Indonesia's worst fire year on record, VIIRS logged 875,824 detections. MODIS logged 178,578.

September or October

Which month burned worst in 2015? MODIS says September. VIIRS says October. Both are right about what they saw.

One record

Count hotspots in MODIS terms: an Aqua hotspot counts 1, a VIIRS hotspot counts 1 divided by a factor for its island group, month and half of the day, about 6 by day and 16 at night. Use one satellite at a time.

11 of 14

On held-out years the sensor gap fell from about 7× to 1.01×, and the two agree on the peak month in 11 of 14 years; the other three are near-ties. Partly converged, and the app says so.

No data is not zero

A day no satellite saw is shown as missing, never as zero. Smoke and cloud can still hide a fire on a day that was observed.

Every light is real

Each glowing point is a 0.05° cell where the harmonised record saw fire: thousands of cells in three months of 2015.

About Bara

Bara is the front door to the Burning Activity Calendar, built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 (Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots). The forest and its fire are an illustration, grown in your browser. The view from orbit is real data: the coastline from the project's province outlines, and every fire light a harmonised 0.05° cell from the project's record for August to October 2015.
